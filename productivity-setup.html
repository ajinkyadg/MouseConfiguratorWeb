Twelve Buttons, Zero Friction

How the right mouse setup changes the way you work — built around MouseConfig's Default preset for the Redragon M913.

← Back to MouseConfig

Most people treat their mouse as a pointer. Click here, scroll there, done. But if your mouse has more than three buttons and you're not using them, you're leaving speed on the table — the kind of speed that doesn't show up as a single dramatic time-save, but as dozens of tiny frictionless moments strung together across a workday.

That's the idea behind MouseConfig's Default preset: a mapping built not around gimmicks, but around the handful of actions everyone does constantly — copying, pasting, switching tabs, jumping between windows, searching, closing things out. Individually, none of these are impressive. Collectively, they're most of what "using a computer" actually is.

Copy and paste, without leaving your hand where it already is

Side buttons 1 and 2 map to Copy and Paste. It sounds almost too simple to matter — until you notice how often your hand already rests near those buttons while you're mid-task, and how that's one less keyboard reach, one less half-second of hunting for Cmd+C, one less break in whatever you were just doing.

Tabs that follow your thumb, not your attention

Side 3 and side 6 move forward and backward through your open tabs. Research, comparison shopping, reading through documentation with six tabs open — this is the mapping that turns tab-hopping from a conscious action into something closer to muscle memory.

Switching windows without breaking stride

Side 4 and side 5 move between windows or spaces. If you work across a browser, a doc, and a chat app in the same ten minutes, this is the difference between a clean handoff and a jarring context switch every time you need to check something.

Search that's one click away

Side 8 triggers Spotlight. Whatever you need — an app, a file, a quick calculation — it's a single press away instead of a keyboard shortcut you have to consciously reach for.

Closing out, cleanly

Side 10 closes the current tab. The small, repetitive cleanup action that happens dozens of times a day, now requiring no more effort than a click.

A wide-angle view when you need it

Side 12 opens Mission Control — the moment you need to see everything at once rather than guess which window has what you're looking for.

None of this is about becoming a power user overnight. It's about noticing that the actions you repeat constantly are exactly the ones worth optimizing, and that a mouse sitting mostly idle under your palm is actually a second input device you've been ignoring.

The part that actually matters, though, isn't the mapping itself — it's how little it costs to set up. Redragon doesn't ship Mac or Linux software at all, which normally means these twelve extra buttons just sit there unused if you're not on Windows. MouseConfig exists specifically to close that gap: no install, no drivers, no admin permissions — you connect, click, and the Default preset above is one click away from being your actual setup. Change anything you don't like just as easily.
