Twelve Buttons, Zero Friction

How the right mouse setup changes the way you work — built around MouseConfig's Productivity presets for the Redragon M913.

Most people treat their mouse as a pointer. Click here, scroll there, done. But if your mouse has more than three buttons and you're not using them, you're leaving speed on the table — the kind of speed that doesn't show up as a single dramatic time-save, but as dozens of tiny frictionless moments strung together across a workday.

That's the idea behind MouseConfig's Productivity presets — one each for macOS, Windows and Linux, since the same action is Cmd+C on one and Ctrl+C on the others. The configurator picks the one for your system automatically. Each is built not around gimmicks, but around the handful of actions everyone does constantly — copying, pasting, switching tabs, undoing, searching, closing things out. Individually, none of these are impressive. Collectively, they're most of what "using a computer" actually is.

Copy and paste, right under your thumb

Side buttons 4 and 5 — the two your thumb rests on — map to Copy and Paste. It sounds almost too simple to matter, until you notice it's one less keyboard reach, one less half-second of hunting for the shortcut, one less break in whatever you were just doing.

Tabs that follow your thumb, not your attention

Side 7 and side 8, just below, move forward and backward through your open tabs. Research, comparison shopping, reading documentation with six tabs open — this turns tab-hopping from a conscious action into muscle memory.

Undo, redo, and a safety net

Side 1 is Undo and side 3 is Redo. Side 2 closes the current tab, and side 10 reopens it — placed on opposite rows so a slip is always one press from being undone.

Search and switching, one click away

Side 6 opens search — Spotlight on macOS, Windows Search, or the GNOME Activities overview. Side 9 switches between applications.

The wide view, and a screenshot

Side 11 takes a screenshot. Side 12 shows everything at once — Mission Control on macOS, Task View on Windows. On Linux, sides 10 and 12 move between workspaces instead.

None of this is about becoming a power user overnight. It's about noticing that the actions you repeat constantly are exactly the ones worth optimizing, and that a mouse sitting mostly idle under your palm is actually a second input device you've been ignoring.

The part that actually matters, though, isn't the mapping itself — it's how little it costs to set up. Redragon doesn't ship Mac or Linux software at all, which normally means these twelve extra buttons just sit there unused if you're not on Windows. MouseConfig exists specifically to close that gap: no install, no drivers, no admin permissions — you connect, click, and the Productivity preset for your system is one click away from being your actual setup. Change anything you don't like just as easily.
