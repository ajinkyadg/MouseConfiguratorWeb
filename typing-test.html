What's a good typing speed?

Most adults type around 40 words per minute. Here is roughly how speeds compare on a 60-second test:

WPMWhat it means
Under 30Still learning; looking at the keys
30–49Average
50–69Above average; comfortable for office work
70–89Fast; enough for most typing-heavy jobs
90–119Very fast
120+Competitive typist

Accuracy matters as much as speed. Below about 95%, the time spent fixing mistakes cancels out the extra speed, so slow down until mistakes are rare.

How the score is worked out

  • WPM counts the characters of words you typed correctly, plus the space after each, divided by five (the standard length of a "word") and by the minutes taken. A word with a mistake in it adds nothing.
  • Raw WPM counts every character you typed, mistakes included. A big gap between raw and WPM means mistakes are costing you speed.
  • Accuracy is correct keystrokes out of all keystrokes. A mistake you fix with Backspace still counts, because the wrong key was pressed.
  • Consistency shows how steady your speed was from second to second. 100% is perfectly even.
  • Most-missed keys are the letters you typed wrong most often: the ones worth practising.

How to type faster

  1. Keep your fingers on the home row (ASDF and JKL;) and return to it after each key.
  2. Look at the screen, not the keyboard. It's slower for a week or two, then much faster.
  3. Aim for accuracy first. Speed follows once the movements are automatic.
  4. Read a word or two ahead, so your fingers never wait for your eyes.
  5. Practise little and often: ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week.

Questions

Which test length should I use?

60 seconds is the usual standard and the best number to compare with other people. 15 and 30 seconds show your burst speed; 120 seconds shows the speed you can keep up.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, but phone keyboards are much slower than physical ones, so phone scores aren't comparable with keyboard scores.

How does "Challenge a friend" work?

It makes a link with your WPM in it. Whoever opens it sees the score to beat. The link carries only that number: no name and no account.

Is anything I type sent anywhere?

No. The test runs in your browser. Only your best score for each length is kept, in your browser's storage.
